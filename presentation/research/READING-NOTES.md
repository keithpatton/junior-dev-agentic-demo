# Reading notes

## What belongs in the 30-minute talk

- A polished first result can be wrong in ways people miss.
- Feedback and adversarial challenge can help when roles and criteria are explicit.
- Debate can also drift; cap the loop and require evidence.
- Context and enforceable invariants make agent work more legible and reliable.
- The developer’s scarce contribution shifts toward intent, system design, and judgment.

## What stays in the repository, not the main talk

- detailed multi-agent debate protocols;
- cross-model voting or consensus choices;
- long-running autonomous-agent harnesses;
- dynamic task graphs and “graph engineering” terminology;
- orchestration, cost, and concurrency mechanics.

These topics are useful follow-on reading, but they compete with the planning and implementation loops for limited audience attention.

## Language discipline

Prefer:

- “structured challenge is a useful control”;
- “the evidence is promising and conditional”;
- “repository context makes decisions legible”;
- “the role expands toward systems leverage.”

Avoid:

- “debate eliminates hallucinations”;
- “independent agents are unbiased”;
- “documentation guarantees correctness”;
- “nobody needs to understand code”;
- “graph engineering has replaced prompt engineering.”
