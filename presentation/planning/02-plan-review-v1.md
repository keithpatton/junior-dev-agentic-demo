# Plan review v1: Beyond the Prompt

## Verdict

`revise lightly, then approve`

This is a challenge pass by the current agent, not an independent reviewer. Keith’s review is the approval gate.

## Criteria used

- one coherent idea for a 30-minute early-career audience;
- alignment with the published talk promise;
- evidence over live-generation spectacle;
- actionable takeaway;
- visual and conceptual simplicity;
- claims calibrated to research;
- role shift feels empowering without dismissing technical knowledge.

## Findings

### 1. Too many metaphors could compete

- Severity: medium
- Evidence: Socrates, spine, bonsai, gardener, two loops, and two directions all appear in 25 minutes.
- Why it matters: the audience may remember imagery but not the method.
- Required resolution: make the two loops the only diagrammatic framework. Socrates explains challenge; gardening explains role and context. “Spine” remains the repository term, not a separate extended metaphor.

### 2. The direction labels can become a terminology argument

- Severity: medium
- Evidence: teams use “horizontal” and “vertical” differently for feature slices and cross-cutting concerns.
- Why it matters: debating axes distracts from Keith’s real insight.
- Required resolution: title the beat **Build up / strengthen across** and name the two modes directly: product growth and system foundations.

### 3. The planning artifacts could become a document tour

- Severity: high
- Evidence: intake, plan, review, and approval each contain substantial text.
- Why it matters: reading Markdown on screen is slow and feels procedural.
- Required resolution: show only one decisive line from each artifact and use the app outcome change as the proof that planning mattered.

### 4. The implementation loop needs one concrete failure

- Severity: high
- Evidence: “challenge the seams” is abstract until the notes boundary is revealed.
- Why it matters: false confidence must be visible, not asserted.
- Required resolution: retain the notes persistence/export failure and the unit-test-passed/browser-broke rehearsal recovery story.

### 5. “Wisdom versus knowledge” needs calibration

- Severity: medium
- Evidence: stated literally, it can imply technical knowledge is no longer important.
- Why it matters: it would conflict with the meetup promise to make juniors stronger engineers.
- Required resolution: use **wisdom over recall** and **code knowledge becomes less sufficient, not irrelevant**.

### 6. Skills are useful but not a talk concept

- Severity: low
- Evidence: frontend, security, documentation, and testing skills can improve both planning and implementation.
- Why it matters: explaining skill systems introduces another layer.
- Required resolution: one speaker-note sentence only: the loops can route to specialist skills when the risk or work type calls for them.

## Residual risks

- 16 beats still require disciplined pacing.
- three image-led slides must not feel like a second visual language.
- the privacy test live option should be cut first if the room interaction runs long.

## Approval recommendation

Approve after Keith confirms or revises:

1. the three audience nuggets;
2. the **Build up / strengthen across** wording;
3. the 25:30 content cut-off;
4. whether the privacy test is live-optional or evidence-only.
