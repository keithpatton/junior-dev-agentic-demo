## Remaining challenge findings

### 1. “The loop” could still sound like a universal prescription

- Severity: medium
- Risk: juniors may apply seven artifacts to every small change.
- Resolution: say explicitly that trivial, reversible work can use a short prompt and test; stronger gates are proportional to ambiguity, consequence, and blast radius.

### 2. A pre-baked example could appear theatrically inevitable

- Severity: medium
- Risk: the audience may suspect the questions were selected only to produce the desired result.
- Resolution: show one genuine discarded or revised assumption from the recorded planning exchange, and label prepared states as rehearsal evidence.

### 3. Native planning comparison must stay fair

- Severity: medium
- Risk: claiming model-native plans disappear or cannot be reviewed would be inaccurate across tools.
- Resolution: say that native capabilities are useful and can run inside the lifecycle. The repo-owned layer adds portability, team ownership, durable context, and tailored gates.

### 4. The context spine could consume too much time

- Severity: medium
- Risk: a file tree tour would stall the narrative.
- Resolution: show the bonsai visual, name the six types of durable context quickly, and open only the privacy invariant that changed during the example.

### 5. The role slide must remain grounded for juniors

- Severity: high
- Risk: “architect/product manager/system thinker” may sound like juniors can skip technical foundations or job boundaries.
- Resolution: frame it as earlier access to broader thinking, not instant seniority. The power comes with responsibility to seek evidence and recognise when specialist judgement is required.

## Final recommendation

Approve the narrative. Keep the TAF deliberately small and describe it as:

> A portable, reviewable record of intent, decisions, evidence, and durable system context around whatever agent is doing the implementation.

Do not add graph engineering, autonomous multi-day agents, or a detailed skills taxonomy to the main talk.
